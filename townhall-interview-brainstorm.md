## Interview Questions & Answers

### 1. The Transformation Moment

**Question:** You went from feeling imposter syndrome on Day 1 to "I got this" by Day 3. What specifically changed? What was the moment or realization that shifted your mindset?

**Answer:**
With an unknown, it's easy to feel "am I behind, am I doing this right?" But then talking to others and seeing what they were doing (the same as me and what we learned that day) made me realize—we're all learning here, it's all good.

Now it's day 6 and I notice myself getting more fluent with my tools. I'm reaching for my AI tools naturally, like I hoped and wrote on day one when the first prompt was "what do you hope to get out of this." Now I have commands like `/learned`, `/smart-commit` etc. to be AI-first.

*Note: The `/learned` command could be a cool one to demo or show a screenshot of. It's cool that it gives me a summary of what I learned today and how it refines my entries.*

---

### 2. The "Full Team in One Person" Reality

**Question:** You learned that "every single person now has the power of a full team at their fingertips." How does this change what's possible for you as a designer? What would have taken a team before that you can now do yourself?

**Answer:**
The fact that I can parse the codebase, understand where things fit, have AI understand the rules and formatting—gives me personal power to build new features single-handedly.

Combine that with my note about collaboration: I can chat with the dev over the real thing on a PR now, vs having to persuade them to develop what I want.

I can also use AI as a product person, especially with context-a8c, to be full stack (product, designer, developer). It's pretty nuts!

---

### 3. The New Source of Truth

**Question:** You captured that collaboration is shifting from "Figma handoff" to "working together on the source of truth—the codebase." How does this change the designer-developer relationship? What does this mean for design at Automattic?

**Answer:**
Personally, I'm excited to work with real code—to get rid of "handoff bugs," the "it doesn't work that way in production code" issues.

So now I'm a closer collaborator with devs than a designer giving them pictures to implement. I can be more active in product development itself than in the bureaucracy of older tools.

This does make me realize that WPUI-lab will eventually need an MCP for this exact reason, since it's a representation of JSX, not the real thing.

---

### 4. The Energy and Possibility

**Question:** You quoted Eric saying he's "more energized and excited about the possibilities of the work we're doing than he has been in years." What possibilities excite YOU most? What are you eager to try when you get back?

**Answer:**
I'm looking forward to full stack—use AI to parse Zendesk and Linear and Slack feedback channels, then be able to execute on those with AI.

I'm looking forward to the full stack designer, who's reaching out to AI as a second reaching hand with commands and skills. Productivity boost!

---

### 5. The Call to Action

**Question:** If you could tell every Automattician one thing about this workshop—one insight that would change how they approach their work tomorrow—what would it be?

**Answer:**
Getting two weeks to learn and practice and synthesize with colleagues via random chats at lunch or breaks is one of the coolest benefits we can get. It's a huge investment in your future self. Of course I know it can be hard for some folks. But what a great opportunity.

Really, this is a glimpse of the future of how we work—it's a pretty dramatic shift too! So it's amazing that Automattic realizes that and is leveraging the opportunity to get ahead by being close and native to today's tools, as James (Director of AI) put it.
